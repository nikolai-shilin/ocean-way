The first thing you notice is the quiet. Not silence, exactly: there's the slap of water against the hulls, a halyard knocking somewhere on the mast, a gull arguing with another gull. But after the airport, the ferry terminal and the taxi with the radio turned all the way up, Paros at seven in the morning feels like someone has finally turned the volume down.

We came for a week, eight of us on a forty-two-foot catamaran, with a skipper named Nikos who has been sailing these waters since he was tall enough to see over the wheel. The plan was loose by design. Sail three or four hours a day, anchor somewhere sheltered, swim, eat, repeat. Nikos decided the route each morning based on the wind, which in the Cyclades in July means the meltemi, the strong northerly that can turn a pleasant crossing into a lively one.

By the second day, nobody was checking their phone. By the third, nobody knew what day it was. By the fourth, a debate broke out over whether we should simply never go home.

On board

A floating apartment with two front porches

The cabins are compact but private, each with its own small bathroom, and the deck is wide enough for eight people to sprawl without anyone touching. The net at the front became the most fought-over spot on the boat, especially at sunset.

Mornings started early. Someone would put the coffee on, someone else would jump straight off the back step into water so clear you could count the stones on the bottom. Lunch was usually at anchor: tomatoes that tasted like tomatoes, feta, bread from whatever bakery we'd found the night before.

The day that stood out

Folegandros was the surprise. Smaller and steeper than its famous neighbour, it has a clifftop village that seems to hang over the sea and almost no traffic. We walked up at dusk, ate grilled octopus in a square lit with strings of bulbs, and walked back down in the dark, following the sound of the water to the harbour.

Questions

Before you go

Do I need sailing experience?

No. The skipper handles the boat. You can help with ropes and the helm if you want to, or just swim and read.

When is the best time to go?

Late May to early October. July and August are windiest; June and September are calmer and quieter.

What should I pack?

Soft bags only, a hat, reef-safe sunscreen and one nice outfit for the evening in Oia.

Can we book the whole boat?

Yes. A full boat of eight gets the per-guest price shown above.
