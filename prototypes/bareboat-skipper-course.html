The idea

What a bareboat skipper actually does

Bareboat means you charter the yacht without a professional skipper or crew. The charter company hands you the keys, and from that moment you are legally responsible for the boat, the people on board and every decision about where and when to go.

To let you do that, charter companies ask for two things: a recognised sailing licence and a short record of your experience, often called a sailing CV. This course gives you both. You spend a week doing the real thing under the eye of an instructor: planning each day, briefing the crew, mooring in busy harbours, anchoring in quiet bays and handling the boat when the wind picks up.

It is not a classroom course with a bit of sailing on the side. Theory is taught on board, at the chart table and in the cockpit, right before you use it. By the last day you skipper the boat yourself while the instructor watches.

Which licence you can get

What you'll learn

Day by day

A week in the Saronic Gulf

The course sails the rycrentals.com Saronic Gulf route from Athens: a loop of about 120 nm, legs of 10–30 nm, a new harbour every evening. Wind is usually 10–18 knots and the meltemi is gentler than in the Cyclades, which makes it a good area to learn. Most town quays have no lazy lines, so you moor stern-to on your own anchor almost every night.

Day five, off Spetses

The night passage

After dinner you slip the lines and sail in the dark: reading lights of other ships, finding the harbour entrance by its light characteristics, keeping a proper lookout. Most students say it's the moment the week comes together.

Get ready

Before you arrive

  1. Read the course book

    We send it after booking. Two evenings are enough for the basics of charts and rules of the road.

  2. Learn five knots

    Bowline, cleat hitch, clove hitch, round turn and two half hitches, figure of eight.

  3. Do the theory online

    Optional, but Day Skipper theory makes the week much easier, and RYA expects it.

  4. Collect your sea miles

    Bring a record of past sailing: dates, boats, miles. It starts your sailing CV.

What to bring

  • Soft bag, no hard suitcase
  • Non-marking deck shoes
  • Sailing gloves
  • Waterproof jacket
  • Warm layer for the night passage
  • Head torch with a red light
  • Sunglasses with a strap, hat
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Seasickness remedy, just in case
  • Passport and a photo for the certificate

After the course

From licence to your first charter

  1. Get your certificate

    Issued on the last day for IYT, or sent by post for RYA within a few weeks.

  2. Add the VHF licence

    A one-day course. Needed in Croatia, useful everywhere.

  3. Start small

    A flotilla or a familiar area with short hops is the best first charter.

  4. Keep logging miles

    Every charter adds to your CV, and opens the way to Coastal Skipper and Yachtmaster.

Questions

Asked before booking

How much experience do I need?

You should know how to steer, tack and handle lines. Around five days on a yacht is ideal. If you're not sure, send us your experience and the instructor will advise.

What happens if I don't pass?

The instructor tells you exactly what to work on. You can finish the assessment on a later course or a shorter training day.

IYT or RYA: which should I choose?

Both are accepted for charter in the Mediterranean. RYA is the route to the ICC for eligible applicants; IYT is issued on the last day and needs no prior theory course.

Do we sleep on the boat?

Yes, all week. Students share double cabins; a private cabin costs extra.

Can my partner come as crew?

Yes, at a reduced crew price, if there's a free berth. They join in everything but aren't assessed.

What about seasickness?

The Saronic Gulf and Croatian coast are sheltered. Bring a remedy anyway; most people are fine after the first day.

How do meals work?

The group shares a food kitty for breakfasts and lunches on board. Dinners are usually ashore.

How soon can I charter on my own?

Right away, but we suggest a first charter in a sheltered area with short hops, and an experienced friend as crew.
