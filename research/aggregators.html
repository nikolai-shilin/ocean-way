Tripadvisor and other aggregators: what a booking costs

Every marketplace that could sell an RYC course or yacht week takes 15–30% of the price. On a €1,590 Bareboat Skipper course that is €240–480 per booking. The useful question is not which platform is cheapest but how much a direct inquiry may cost before an aggregator becomes the better deal.

Summary

Five things to know

Tripadvisor is Viator

Bookable "things to do" on Tripadvisor are fulfilled by Viator at Viator's commission (20–30%, typically 25%). The free Tripadvisor listing is still worth having for reviews and trust.

Commission is 15–30% everywhere

Lowest: Click&Boat (15–18% plus a renter fee) and TourRadar (~19–20% all in). Highest: Viator, GetYourGuide, Sputnik8 (24%+). Viator and GYG let you pay more for better ranking.

A direct inquiry may cost €40–80

If RYC turns 10–20% of inquiries into bookings, a direct lead costing under €40–80 beats a 25% platform on the €1,590 course. Above that, the platform is cheaper.

The course ladder changes the maths

A student who starts on the €990 entry course via an OTA (€248 commission) and then books the next two courses direct costs 5.7% of €4,370 lifetime revenue. Platforms are a good first step and an expensive habit.

Resold charters don't fit

Boat marketplaces take 15–20%. If RYC's agency margin on a fleet charter is in that range too (to confirm), listing resold boats leaves nothing. Use them only for RYC's own products: courses, cabin-based yacht weeks.

Break-even calculator

When is a direct lead cheaper than the platform?

Break-even cost per inquiry = platform commission per booking × your inquiry-to-booking rate. If a direct inquiry costs less than that, selling direct is cheaper.

Recommendations

What to do

1. Claim the free Tripadvisor listing now; skip Business Advantage

It costs nothing, collects reviews, and gives the brand a trust signal for buyers who check Tripadvisor before booking direct. Bookable listings there come through Viator anyway.

2. Test TourRadar for the courses and yacht weeks

It is built for multi-day trips and charges the least among experience platforms (~19–20% all in). One season of listings for two or three course dates gives real conversion data.

3. Use Viator/GYG for a day sail, not the full course

Their buyers look for day activities. A "taster day" sells to that audience and feeds the course ladder; the full course would sit among day trips at 25% commission.

4. For Russian-speaking buyers, try Sputnik8 or Tripster with care

Audience fit is good, but Sputnik8 starts at 24% and Tripster requires the same price as on RYC's own site, so the commission can't be passed on. Confirm Tripster's rate in the guide account first.

5. Keep resold fleet charters off boat marketplaces

Click&Boat, SamBoat and Boataround already list the same fleets. List only RYC's own products there, such as cabin-based yacht weeks with an RYC skipper.

6. Turn platform customers into direct customers

After the trip, offer the next course on the ladder through RYC's own channels. Check each platform's terms first: most forbid moving the original booking off-platform.

7. Start measuring cost per inquiry and booking rate

Every number in this report is a benchmark until RYC tracks its own cost per inquiry, inquiry → booking rate and source of each booking. The calculator above then gives the answer per channel.
